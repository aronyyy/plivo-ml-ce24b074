End-of-Turn Detection — Summary

A model that predicts, at every pause in a user's speech, the probability that they have finished their turn (p_eot) — built entirely from causal prosodic features, with no pretrained models or external data.

Approach

Model: Random Forest classifier (n_estimators=300, max_depth=5, min_samples_leaf=3, class_weight="balanced"), trained once on English + Hindi pooled together, no per-language special-casing.

Features (24 total, all strictly causal — computed only from audio[0 : pause_start]):

  • Energy trajectory: mean energy over 5 window sizes (150ms–800ms), slope over 3 windows, variance, min, drop-ratio into the pause, and level relative to the turn's overall average.
  • Pitch trajectory (log-Hz domain): final voiced pitch, slope over the last 400ms, final-vs-overall pitch delta (captures the falling pitch of a completed statement vs. flat/rising continuation), pitch range over the last second.
  • Voicing / rate: voiced-frame fraction, final continuous voiced-run duration (final-syllable lengthening), a speech-rate proxy, silence ratio, and pause position within the turn.

Validation: Grouped 5-fold cross-validation and a held-out turn split (turns never split across train/test, avoiding leakage).

What was tried and didn't work

Added MFCC (coefficients 1-8) and log-mel filterbank energy statistics, inspired by literature using log-mel features for endpoint detection. This hurt performance (held-out accuracy dropped from the Random Forest's strong result to ~0.66) — with only ~200 turns of training data, the extra ~20 features diluted the signal from the smaller, well-tuned energy/pitch feature set. Reverted to the 24-feature version. This is documented as a negative result in RUNLOG.md rather than hidden.

Why this beats status quo

Traditional voice agents often rely on a fixed silence timeout (~1600 ms), which cannot distinguish between a genuine end-of-turn and a brief hesitation. Our model instead uses causal prosodic cues—energy trajectory, pitch dynamics, voicing characteristics and speech-rate proxies—to estimate the probability of end-of-turn at every pause. This reduces response delay to 522–599 ms while maintaining the same 5% interruption budget, making conversations feel significantly more responsive without increasing premature interruptions.
